## About SADMS

The South Asia Drought Monitoring System addresses the existing and potential
challenges to drought management and provides a framework for proactive drought
mitigation across South Asia. It gives national authorities the maximum possible
lead time to activate mitigation strategies and reduce vulnerability to drought.

It provides farmers, extension workers, and agriculture and water resources
authorities with the information needed to forecast, monitor and manage drought:
seasonal, sub-seasonal and seven-day weather forecasts; monitoring tools that
indicate when drought is present and at what severity; and district-level
agricultural contingency plans that can be put into action once triggers are
reached.

The system covers **Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Pakistan and
Sri Lanka**.

## History

SADMS was originally launched in **2014**. The next-generation platform followed
in **2022**, developed in close partnership with the **Indian Council of
Agricultural Research (ICAR)**.

Since 2014 the system has guided national, state and district-level authorities
in India and Sri Lanka to act ahead of drought, helping cushion smallholders from
the worst effects of prolonged dry spells. IWMI received the **Geospatial World
Excellence Award 2020** for the remote-sensing work behind it.

## Relationship to the S2S programme

SADMS is one of the delivery platforms the programme's forecast products flow
into. It receives district-level drought triggers derived from downscaled S2S
forecasts, at a 2–3 week lead.

It is also the drought-monitoring backbone that
[SukhaRakshak AI]({{ site.baseurl }}/tools/sukharakshak-ai/) draws on to generate
farmer-level advisories.
